You're Probably Towel-Drying Wrong — and It Costs You Ten Minutes

The rough head-rub feels productive. It's the single most damaging thing most people do to wet hair — and it doesn't even save time. The fix takes sixty seconds.

By The Hair Reviewed Editors· October 20, 2026· 3 min read

Blot and squeeze — never rub. Photo: Unsplash

Here's a sentence that saves hair and time in equal measure: the towel's job is to remove water, not to dry your hair. Those are different jobs, and the vigorous rub does the first one badly while quietly doing real damage.

Why the rub is the problem

Wet hair is hair at its weakest — water swells the strand and loosens its structure, which is why wet hair snaps and tangles so easily. Rubbing a terry towel back and forth across it drags thousands of tiny loops against a raised, fragile cuticle. The result is the frizz halo, the mid-length breakage, and the tangles you then fight with a brush. The rough-dry doesn't just damage; it undoes the smoothing your conditioner just did.

The sixty-second version that works

Squeeze, don't scrub. Press sections of hair between towel-covered palms, squeeze down the length in the direction the hair grows, then wrap it and let the towel sit while you do your skincare. A minute of squeezing plus a few minutes wrapped removes more water than any amount of rubbing — with zero friction. An old cotton t-shirt or a microfiber towel does it even more gently than terry cloth; the smoother surface is the whole trick, and the t-shirt is free.

The ten minutes you get back

Every drop the towel removes is a drop the dryer doesn't have to. Hair that's been properly squeezed and wrapped starts the blow-dry closer to damp than soaked, which can cut drying time by a third or more — less time under heat, less time holding your arm up, less of the close-range blasting that actually causes heat damage. The gentlest step in the routine is also the one that shortens all the others.

The routine, in one line

Squeeze with the towel, wrap for five minutes, then dry with heat — moving, at a distance, down to bone dry. Your hair spends the least possible time in its fragile wet state, and your morning spends the least possible time on it.
