What the Dyson wins

Credit where due. The Supersonic's motor and heat control set the standard the whole category now chases, the attachment ecosystem is enormous, and you can walk into almost any store and touch one before buying. If a dryer is jewelry to you — a brand you want on the shelf — Dyson wins that contest before it starts.

What the Pleume wins

Everything that happens during the 20 to 30 minutes the thing is actually running. The Dyson, like every other dryer ever made, needs your hand overhead for all of it. The Pleume stands on its tripod or mounts to the wall and dries while your hands do something else — the one capability no amount of Dyson engineering has addressed. Under the hood it's the same idea: a 100,000+ RPM brushless motor and 50 million+ negative ions, so the silky, frizz-free finish is the same. The differences are the ones you can see: brushed aluminum where the Dyson is plastic, 20 cm instead of full-size, and $230 less.

So who buys which?

Buy the Dyson if you love the ritual of styling, want the attachment wardrobe, and the price isn't a factor. Buy the Pleume if drying your hair is a chore you'd rather not give an arm to — if your mornings are a race, your shoulder complains, or you've ever tried to answer a text with a dryer in one hand. Those are two different people, and only one machine exists for the second one.
