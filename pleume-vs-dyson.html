What the Dyson wins

Credit where due. The Supersonic's motor and heat control set the standard the whole category now chases, the attachment ecosystem is enormous, the warranty is a year longer, and you can walk into almost any store and touch one before buying. If a dryer is jewelry to you — a brand you want on the shelf — Dyson wins that contest before it starts.

What the Pleume wins

Everything that happens during the 20 to 30 minutes the thing is actually running. The Dyson, like every other dryer ever made, needs your hand overhead for all of it. The Pleume stands on its base or mounts to the wall and dries while your hands do something else — the one capability no amount of Dyson engineering has addressed. It's also aluminum where the Dyson is plastic, packs to a fraction of the size, and costs $230 less. Same motor class, negative-ion airflow, from a smaller body.

So who buys which?

Buy the Dyson if you love the ritual of styling, want the attachment wardrobe, and the price isn't a factor. Buy the Pleume if drying your hair is a chore you'd rather not give an arm to — if your mornings are a race, your shoulder complains, or you've ever tried to answer a text with a dryer in one hand. Those are two different people, and only one machine exists for the second one.
