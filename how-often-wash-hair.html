How Often Should You Actually Wash Your Hair?

Somewhere between "every day" and "never, use rice water" is a boring, correct answer. It depends on exactly two things — and neither of them is written on the shampoo bottle.

By The Hair Reviewed Editors· Updated October 2026· 3 min read

The internet has turned wash frequency into a personality trait. It isn't. It's a scheduling question with two inputs.

Input one: your scalp, not your hair

Shampoo exists to clean the scalp; the lengths mostly come along for the ride. Oily scalps genuinely do better washed more often — stretching washes doesn't "train" oil glands, that's a myth that refuses to die. Fine, straight hair shows oil fastest and usually lands at every day or two. Thick, curly and coily hair carries oil down the strand slowly and can comfortably go several days to a week, with co-washing in between. Your scalp sets the floor.

Input two: what the week does to it

Sweat, styling products, and city air all shorten the clock. A heavy gym week is a wash-more week regardless of hair type; a slicked-back-bun week stretches it. Dry shampoo buys a day, not three — it absorbs oil at the roots but cleans nothing, and a scalp full of powder eventually itches its way to the front of your mind.

Finding your number

Start from your type's baseline — daily-ish for fine and oily, two to three days for most straight-to-wavy hair, longer for curls and coils — then adjust one day at a time until your scalp is neither greasy nor tight. That's it. The right schedule is the one where you stop thinking about your scalp between washes.
